\section{{Overview of Stroke Rehabilitation}}

Stroke occurs when the blood supply to a part of the brain is interrupted or when bleeding damages
brain tissue. It can lead to neurological problems, including weakness, impaired coordination, reduced
balance and difficulty performing daily activities. Motor impairment is one of the major challenges faced
by stroke survivors.
Rehabilitation plays an important role in helping patients regain functional abilities after a stroke.
Conventional rehabilitation includes physical exercises, balance training, gait training and other
therapeutic activities designed to improve movement and independence. However, the amount of
recovery varies because of differences in initial impairment, clinical condition and individual
characteristics.
Brain–Computer Interface (BCI)-based rehabilitation is an emerging approach that uses brain activity to
identify a patient’s intention to move. When the system recognizes the intended movement, it can
activate an external device, such as a rehabilitation robot, to assist the movement. This approach aims
to connect the patient’s intention with physical movement and provide feedback during rehabilitation.
Despite its potential, BCI-based rehabilitation does not produce the same outcome for every patient.
Some patients show substantial improvement, while others experience limited gains. Predicting these
differences before or during treatment could help clinicians understand individual recovery potential
and plan appropriate interventions.
The selected research paper addresses this challenge using multimodal behavioral data and machine
learning. It combines demographic, clinical and biomechanical characteristics to predict post-treatment
lower-limb motor function and classify patients according to their response to BCI therapy. Explainable
AI methods are used to identify the features that contribute to the predictions.\cite{bib5}


\section{Motivation and Importance}
The motivation for this research comes from the need to improve the effectiveness of stroke
rehabilitation through individualized treatment planning. A conventional rehabilitation strategy may not
be equally effective for every patient. Clinical assessments provide useful information, but a single
measurement may not capture the complete relationship between a patient’s condition and potential
response to treatment.
Machine learning can identify patterns among multiple patient characteristics and use these patterns to
estimate rehabilitation outcomes. However, a prediction alone is not sufficient in a clinical setting.
Healthcare professionals also need to understand why a model produces a particular result.
Explainable AI addresses this requirement by identifying important features and showing how they
contribute to model predictions. The combination of multimodal data, predictive models and
explainability may therefore support more informed rehabilitation planning.\cite{bib1}
