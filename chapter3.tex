\section{Problem Addressed}
Patients recovering from stroke show considerable variation in their response to BCI-based
rehabilitation. Some achieve meaningful improvements in lower-limb motor function, while others show
limited recovery. A treatment strategy that works well for one patient may not produce the same result
for another.
Traditional clinical assessment may not capture all relationships among demographic, clinical and
biomechanical factors. Furthermore, machine-learning models may generate predictions without clearly
identifying the features that influenced their outputs.
Problem statement: How can multimodal behavioral data be used to predict lower-limb motor recovery
and classify stroke patients’ response to BCI-based rehabilitation while explaining the factors
influencing these predictions?\cite{bib1}
\section{Significance of the Problem}
Accurate prediction of rehabilitation outcomes could help clinicians establish realistic treatment goals
and identify patients who may need additional assessment or alternative strategies. A model using
multiple types of patient information may capture complementary relationships that are not
represented by one clinical measurement alone.
Explainability is equally important. Identifying influential variables such as baseline motor impairment,
muscle spasticity and balance function can help clinicians understand the basis of model predictions.
The framework therefore addresses both prediction and explanation.
\section{Scope of the Seminar}
This seminar reviews the research framework proposed by Sun et al. for predicting stroke patients’
response to BCI-based rehabilitation using explainable AI. It covers BCI rehabilitation, multimodal
patient data, regression, classification, LOOCV, stepwise regression, SHAP, reported performance,
applications, limitations and future research.
This is a review of the published research. It does not claim that new patient data were collected or that
the original clinical experiments were independently reproduced.
