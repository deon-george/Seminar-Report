\section{URL-Only Detection Methods}
URL-only methods derive lexical, structural, and domain-related features from the address itself and apply classifiers such as logistic regression, decision trees, random forests, gradient-boosted trees, or neural networks. They are practical because they can analyse a URL before fully loading the destination page. Reviews cited by Misiek and Hyla describe a broad range of URL-based features and classifiers, while also noting that within-dataset evaluations do not necessarily demonstrate generalisation.\cite{bib1}

\section{Neural and Embedding-Based Methods}
GramBeddings uses character-level and n-gram representations in a neural approach to identify phishing URLs. Other research combines lexical URL characteristics with neural architectures such as DNNs and LSTMs. These approaches can model complex character patterns, but may require additional computational resources and can still be affected by differences between datasets.\cite{bib1}

\section{LLM-Based and Hybrid Approaches}
More recent research investigates large language models and ensembles for phishing detection, as well as hybrid systems that combine URLs with page content, HTML, or other signals. Such methods can add contextual information but may increase computational cost, latency, and deployment complexity. They are therefore not automatically preferable for a lightweight browser extension.\cite{bib1}

\section{Domain Adaptation and Cross-Dataset Research}
Domain-adaptation research attempts to reduce differences between source and target datasets. The selected paper highlights prior findings that F1 scores can decline by 10--32\% under dataset shift and argues that evaluation across independent sources is essential. Its contribution is a systematic cross-dataset evaluation of an XGBoost model together with criteria for selecting stable features.\cite{bib1}

\section{Comparison with the Selected Paper}
Compared with the authors' earlier conference version, the journal paper expands evaluation to a custom dataset, GramBeddings, and PhiUSIIL; adds broader cross-dataset experiments and threshold analysis; applies explicit cross-dataset feature-selection criteria; uses Bayesian hyperparameter optimisation; and reports more detailed performance and computational comparisons.\cite{bib1}
