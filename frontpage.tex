\begin{center}
  \vspace*{1cm}
  \textbf{\large ABSTRACT}
\end{center}
{\normalsize
\begin{spacing}{1.0}
Brain--Computer Interface (BCI) technology offers a promising approach to improving lower-limb motor recovery after stroke, but patients respond differently to BCI therapy. Predicting each patient's response, measured as improvement in lower-limb motor function, is therefore important. This study presents an Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) framework that predicts stroke rehabilitation outcomes from multimodal behavioral data. It uses a private clinical dataset of 42 subacute stroke patients drawn from a larger randomized controlled trial of 167 patients. Before rehabilitation, the study collected demographic information (age, gender, stroke type, and time since stroke), clinical assessments (lower-limb motor function, muscle spasticity, balance, walking ability, and activities of daily living), and biomechanical measurements (ankle joint range of motion and muscle strength). Elastic Net (EN) and Artificial Neural Network (ANN) models analyzed these data to predict outcomes after two weeks of BCI therapy. SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) identified the factors most influential to recovery. Baseline motor function, muscle spasticity, and balance ability emerged as the strongest predictors of successful rehabilitation. Compared with single-variable prediction models, the multimodal approach achieved higher prediction accuracy while offering transparent, interpretable insights for clinical decision-making. The findings demonstrate that XAI can support personalized rehabilitation planning by helping clinicians identify patients most likely to benefit from BCI therapy and optimize treatment strategies to improve recovery outcomes.

\end{spacing}
}
