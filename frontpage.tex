\begin{center}
  \vspace*{1cm}
  \textbf{\large ABSTRACT}
\end{center}
\begin{spacing}{2.0}
Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) achieve photo-realistic novel view synthesis, but their performance degrades significantly when trained on real-world multi-view images that contain blur, noise, and misalignment. While existing methods like Deblur-NeRF utilize kernel deformation to improve sharpness, their overall results are negatively impacted by the quantity of blurry training samples and inherent data imbalance. This report introduces Neural Radiance Fields Deblurring with Active Learning (NeRF-DA), which focuses on identifying high-quality blurred images for 3D scene modeling to overcome these limitations. NeRF-DA employs a pool-based active learning strategy combined with uncertainty estimation to intelligently curate an optimal training set and enhance model efficiency. The framework achieves this by designing the model through the calculation of camera pose distance (to ensure data balance) and entropy for blurred images (to select informative data). Furthermore, NeRF-DA overcomes static dataset limitations by incorporating unseen view camera poses into the training ray selection. Experimental results involving both camera motion blur and defocus blur demonstrate that NeRF-DA significantly enhances the quality of existing Deblur-NeRF reconstructions and improves the accuracy of 3D scene reconstruction.
\end{spacing}
